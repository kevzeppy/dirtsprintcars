From Speedcar Champion to Two-Time WA Sprintcar Champion: Dayne Kingshott’s Rise With Krikke Motorsport

The Bunbury driver won his second Western Australian Sprintcar Championship on Nov. 7, 2026, at his team’s home track, four seasons after his first and nearly eight years after winning the Australian Speedcar Championship.

Published November 10, 2026 · DirtSprintCars.com

Dayne Kingshott won the Western Australian Sprintcar Championship on Nov. 7, 2026, at Bunbury Speedway, leading every lap of the 35-lap feature after starting second, according to Auto Action. It was his second state title, Krikke Motorsport’s ninth and the team’s first win of the 2026-27 season. Callum Williamson finished second, Kaiden Manders third, Kris Coyle fourth and Daniel Harding fifth. The race was round three of the Maddington Toyota Sprintcar Series, and Speedway Illustrated News reported in July that it was the first time in some while the event had been run at the South West venue. Kingshott, who drives the WA No. 2 Mobil 1/Bunbury Toyota-backed Maxim, said the win meant more because it was the team’s first together at Bunbury, where many of its sponsors are local, the team said.

His route to the title began in speedcars. The Bunbury resident won the Australian Speedcar Championship at Perth Motorplex on Feb. 9, 2019, ahead of Adam Clarke of New South Wales and American Jason McDougal. He moved to 410 Sprintcars with Krikke Motorsport for 2020-21, when pandemic travel restrictions kept Kerry Madsen from driving the team’s car, and finished second in the SEG Series standings that season, according to the team’s history page. He won his first 410 Sprintcar feature with the team in 2021-22, along with the Perth Motorplex Track Championship.

His first state title came on Dec. 3, 2022, in the 61st running of the event at Perth Motorplex. Starting third, Kingshott took the lead from James Inglis with about six laps to go in a 35-lap feature that took more than an hour because of repeated stoppages, and finished ahead of Taylor Milling and Inglis. Krikke Motorsport called it the team’s eighth Western Australian title, following Ron Krikke in 1983-84 and 1984-85, John Krikke in 1988-89, Brooke Tatnell in 2013-14, Jamie Veal in 2017-18 and Madsen in 2018-19 and 2019-20.

The state titles sit beside a run of consistent seasons. Kingshott won the Maddington Toyota Sprintcar Series in 2023-24 with 2,820 points, the first series championship for Krikke Motorsport, after finishing second, third and fourth in the previous three seasons. He repeated in 2024-25 with 3,222 points, 52 ahead of Williamson, and won a third series title in 2025-26 with six round wins. In November 2025 he became the first driver to win the King of Wings three years in a row, beating James McFadden by 0.010 seconds at Perth Motorplex. In December, at the 2025 High Limit International at Perth Motorplex, he finished second to Kyle Larson in the $110,000-to-win race.

Among Western Australian champions, Kingshott now has two titles. Perth Motorplex credits Alf Barbagallo, known as “Mr. Sprintcar,” with six, and the Sprintcar World honour roll, which begins in 2009, lists Jason Kendrick (2011, 2014 and 2021) and Madsen (2010, 2018 and 2019) with three each. Kingshott sits seventh in the 2026-27 series standings, and Krikke Motorsport has said it is aiming for a fourth Maddington Toyota Sprintcar Series championship. The next round is the King of Wings at Perth Motorplex on Nov. 14, where Kingshott is the three-time defending winner.
