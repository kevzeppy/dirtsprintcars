Jason Johnson’s 2016 Knoxville Nationals Upset Ended Donny Schatz's Reign by 0.168 Seconds

The Eunice, La., driver beat the nine-time champion in the Priority Aviation No. 41 to win a crown jewel and the $150,000 first prize at the 56th Nationals.

Published November 15, 2026 · DirtSprintCars.com

On Aug. 13, 2016, Jason Johnson of Eunice, La., beat Donny Schatz by 0.168 seconds to win the 56th 5-Hour Energy Knoxville Nationals presented by Casey’s General Stores, a crown jewel, and the $150,000 first prize. The official World of Outlaws recap, titled “Cajun-Style Upset,” said the result denied Schatz a 10th Nationals win. Schatz, already a nine-time champion of the event, started fifth. Johnson started second in the Priority Aviation No. 41 Maxim. Shane Stewart finished third, pole-sitter Daryn Pittman was fourth and Kyle Larson was fifth after starting 21st. Larson was the hard charger.

Johnson earned the outside front row after a runner-up finish on preliminary Night 2 made him the high-point driver of that session, the World of Outlaws reported. Kerry Madsen and Jamie Veal started third and fourth. Pittman led the first 10 laps of the 50-lap A-main. Official lap leaders show Johnson in front on lap 11, Pittman back in front on lap 12 and Schatz taking the lead on lap 13. Schatz still led when a mandatory caution came at halfway and through lap 26.

On the second-half restart, Johnson used the cushion, took the lead on lap 27 and nearly climbed the wall on lap 34, according to the official recap. Schatz regained the lead on laps 44 and 45. The two traded slide jobs over the final segment, and the World of Outlaws said they made contact, before Johnson led laps 46 through 50. “I gave it my all,” Johnson said. “I wanted to win just as bad as Donny wanted to win.” Schatz said Johnson “did one hell of a job” and “earned it.”

In victory lane Johnson said, “Parked it, BC,” a dedication T.J. Buffenbarger recorded for TJSlideways.com. Bryan Clauson had died on Aug. 7, 2016, of injuries from a crash the night before at the Belleville (Kan.) Nationals, Indianapolis Motor Speedway reported. The official World of Outlaws recap also quoted Johnson calling the win “a testament to friendship, to what life is all about, and to B.C.”

The finish stood out against the series standings. After the race, unofficial World of Outlaws points listed Johnson seventh with two feature wins, 12 top-fives and 31 top-10s. Schatz led the championship with 16 wins. Buffenbarger called the Nationals victory an upset and wrote that Johnson had not won a World of Outlaws feature since March 23 at Placerville Speedway and that his best finish leading into the event was fifth on Aug. 5 at Federated Auto Parts Raceway at I-55 in Pevely, Mo.

Johnson died of injuries from a crash on June 23, 2018, during the Jim Boyd Memorial at Beaver Dam Raceway in Wisconsin. He was 41. A World of Outlaws memorial later credited him with 12 series feature wins, five American Sprint Car Series championships and the 2016 Knoxville Nationals title, his only win in that crown jewel. The series still runs the Jason Johnson Classic in his memory. Carson Macedo won the 2026 edition on Oct. 23 at Texarkana 67 Speedway in the Jason Johnson Racing No. 41.
