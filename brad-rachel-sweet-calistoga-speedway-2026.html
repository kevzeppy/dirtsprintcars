Brad and Rachel Sweet Picked to Operate Calistoga Speedway, Pending Council Approval

Their proposal received the highest score in the city’s operator selection process. The license agreement still needs Calistoga City Council approval, tentatively expected Dec. 8.

Published November 14, 2026 · DirtSprintCars.com

The Calistoga Fairgrounds announced Nov. 13 that it has signed a letter of intent to move forward with Brad and Rachel Sweet as operators of Calistoga Speedway, the half-mile track in California’s Napa Valley. The fairgrounds said the Sweets’ proposal received the highest score in the speedway operator selection process.

The proposed license agreement still needs approval from the Calistoga City Council before it can take effect, according to the announcement. Council consideration is tentatively expected Dec. 8, 2026. The city’s request for proposals, released Sept. 4, had set a Nov. 2 deadline for proposals and called for an operator to “promote, operate, improve and maintain” motorized racing at the speedway, with a Jan. 1 start date and at least three sprint car weekends a season, Calistoga Currents reported.

Brad Sweet, of Grass Valley, California, is a former World of Outlaws champion and co-owner of High Limit Racing with Kyle Larson. He has three World of Outlaws wins at Calistoga, in 2014, 2015 and 2017, according to the World of Outlaws, and has promoted Outlaws events at Placerville Speedway.

The city bought the fairgrounds from Napa County in July 2024 for $2 million. The track did not host sprint car racing from 2020 until the Louie Vermeil Classic returned in 2025. Tommy Hunt, whose HMC Promotions has run the Vermeil Classic since 2008, announced in September that the 2026 edition would be his family’s last, Calistoga Currents reported. Racing at the fairgrounds dates to 1937.
