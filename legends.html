World of Outlaws

Founded in 1978 by Ted Johnson, the World of Outlaws turned winged 410 sprint car racing into a national touring series, running coast to coast nearly every weekend from February to November.

Steve Kinser"The King"

Steve Kinser is the winningest driver in World of Outlaws history, with 20 WoO championships and 690 feature wins. "The King" won the inaugural 1978 Outlaws title, a record 12 Knoxville Nationals and seven Kings Royals.

Steve Kinser built the World of Outlaws into a national institution as much as any promoter did. Arriving for the series' first season in 1978, the Bloomington, Indiana, driver and his cousin Karl Kinser's team set the standard every touring sprint car program has chased since, and the National Sprint Car Hall of Fame later named him sprint car racing's greatest driver of the century.

He won the inaugural 1978 Outlaws title and 20 series championships in all. His 690 World of Outlaws A-feature wins are nearly 300 more than anyone else, a margin unmatched in American motorsports.

Kinser owned the sport's crown jewels. He won the Knoxville Nationals a record 12 times and the Kings Royal at Eldora seven times, and among the majors he won the Gold Cup Race of Champions at Chico more than ten times.

His 1987 season remains the benchmark. He won 46 Outlaws features that year, including 24 of the final 26.

Kinser was versatile beyond the dirt. He won the 1994 IROC race at Talladega, started the 1995 Daytona 500 and drove in the 1997 Indianapolis 500.

He briefly left the Outlaws for the National Sprint Tour in 2006, returned in 2007, and retired from sprint car racing in 2016. He was inducted into the National Sprint Car Hall of Fame in 2005 and the Motorsports Hall of Fame of America in 2017. His son Kraig, himself a 2005 Knoxville Nationals winner, carried the family name forward.

Sammy Swindell"Slammin' Sammy"

Sammy Swindell is a three-time World of Outlaws champion (1981, 1982, 1997) whose 394 WoO feature wins rank second all-time. He also won the 1983 Knoxville Nationals, three Kings Royals and five Chili Bowl Midget Nationals, and in 1997 he became the first owner-driver to win the Outlaws title.

Sammy Swindell was the engineer of the modern sprint car as much as its driver. A technically minded Tennessean from a racing family, he was one of the Memphis-area group that helped make the early World of Outlaws, and alongside Steve Kinser and Doug Wolfgang he is remembered as one of the "Big Three" of winged sprint car racing.

He won Outlaws championships in 1981 and 1982. In 1997 he won a third title in his own car, becoming the first owner-driver to win the WoO crown. He was also runner-up four times, and his 394 World of Outlaws A-main wins rank second all-time.

Swindell won the 1983 Knoxville Nationals and the Kings Royal in 1992, 1999 and 2012. In 1981 he took the wing off to win the Pacific Coast Nationals at Ascot.

In midgets he won the Chili Bowl five times: 1989, 1992, 1996, 1998 and 2009.

Swindell also tested himself elsewhere. He made CART starts in 1985 and 1986, failed to qualify for the 1987 Indianapolis 500, and ran the inaugural 1995 NASCAR Truck Series season, finishing 12th in points.

He was inducted into the National Sprint Car Hall of Fame in 2006.

Donny Schatz

Donny Schatz is a ten-time World of Outlaws champion, second only to Steve Kinser, and an 11-time Knoxville Nationals winner. Driving for Tony Stewart/Curb-Agajanian Racing from 2008, he also won six Kings Royals and a record six Williams Grove National Opens.

Donny Schatz built the most sustained championship program of the World of Outlaws' modern era. The Fargo, North Dakota, driver was the series' 1997 Rookie of the Year, won his first Outlaws feature in 1998, and joined Tony Stewart's team in 2008. That partnership with Tony Stewart/Curb-Agajanian Racing ran 18 seasons and produced most of his titles.

Schatz won ten World of Outlaws championships: 2006 through 2009, 2012, and 2014 through 2018. Only Steve Kinser has won more. He has more than 300 Outlaws feature wins, which rank third all-time.

He won the Knoxville Nationals 11 times, in 2006–09, 2011–15, 2017 and 2022, one short of Kinser's record. He also won six Kings Royals and a record six National Opens at Williams Grove.

Fans know him for his consistency across a full season and his command of sprint car racing's crown-jewel events.

Doug Wolfgang

Doug Wolfgang of Sioux Falls, South Dakota, is a five-time Knoxville Nationals champion with 140 World of Outlaws feature wins, fifth-most all-time. He was one of the "Big Three" of the early Outlaws era, and he also won two Kings Royals and 37 All Star Circuit of Champions races.

Doug Wolfgang carried the Midwest's homegrown sprint car scene onto the national stage. The Sioux Falls, South Dakota, driver came up in super modifieds and, with car owner Bob Trostle, broke through at Knoxville in the mid-1970s before becoming one of the "Big Three" of the early World of Outlaws era.

Wolfgang won the Knoxville Nationals five times, in 1977, 1978, 1984, 1985 and 1989. He won them for four different owners: Trostle, Bill Smith, Bob Weikert and Danny Peace.

With the Outlaws he finished second in the standings four times and won 140 features, fifth-most in series history. He also won 37 All Star Circuit of Champions races and two Kings Royals.

On April 3, 1992, a fiery crash at Lakeside Speedway in Kansas City left him with burns over about 30 percent of his body and a broken vertebra. He came back to win an IMCA 360 sprint championship and the 1996 ASCS 360 Nationals, then retired in 1997 after another serious crash.

Wolfgang is a member of the National Sprint Car Hall of Fame and later built sprint car chassis from his shop near Sioux Falls.

High Limit Racing

Brad Sweet and Kyle Larson launched High Limit Racing in 2022, and it became a full touring series in 2023. Interstate Batteries is now its title sponsor. The series is young, but it has already become a major stage for midweek and crown-jewel winged sprint car racing.

Rico Abreu

Rico Abreu is the 2025 High Limit Racing champion and the series' all-time leader in wins, starts, podiums and top-10 finishes. He also won the 2014 USAC National Midget championship and back-to-back Chili Bowl Midget Nationals in 2015 and 2016.

Rico Abreu has been part of High Limit Racing from its start and helped build it into a national tour. Abreu is a Northern Californian who has achondroplasia and is four feet four inches tall. He has run every High Limit points race since 2023 and holds the series' career records for wins, starts, podiums and top-10 finishes.

He made his name in midgets. He won the 2014 USAC National Midget championship, then won the Chili Bowl Midget Nationals in back-to-back years, 2015 and 2016.

Stock cars came next, with backing from his friend Kyle Larson. He won the 2015 NASCAR K&N Pro Series East race at Columbus Motor Speedway, his first stock car win, before returning to open-wheel racing.

Back on dirt, he became a regular winner at marquee events, including the Outlaws and High Limit shows at Eldora's 4-Crown. He won his first national sprint car championship, the 2025 High Limit title, by winning the season finale at Texas Motor Speedway.

USAC Sprint Cars

USAC's National Sprint Car championship dates to 1956. It is non-winged 410 racing, and it has long been a proving ground for drivers headed to Indianapolis and NASCAR. A USAC career Triple Crown means a driver has won the national championship in all three USAC divisions: Sprint, Midget and Silver Crown. Only eight drivers have done it.

Pancho Carter

Pancho Carter was USAC's first career Triple Crown champion. He won the 1972 National Midget title, the 1974 and 1976 Sprint Car titles and the 1978 Silver Crown title. He also made 17 Indianapolis 500 starts, won the 1985 Indy pole and won the 1981 Michigan 500.

Pancho Carter set the template for USAC versatility. The son of USAC Hall of Famer Duane Carter, he became the organization's first career Triple Crown champion and showed a generation of short-track drivers that one racer could master midgets, sprints and Silver Crown cars.

He won the 1972 USAC National Midget title and Sprint Car titles in 1974 and 1976. In 1978 he won the Silver Crown title, then called the USAC National Dirt Championship. He scored 70 USAC national feature wins across those series.

Carter was the 1974 Indianapolis 500 Rookie of the Year and made 17 Indy 500 starts. He won the pole in 1985 and finished third in 1982. He also won the 1981 Michigan 500.

He is a member of both the National Sprint Car and National Midget Halls of Fame and the USAC Hall of Fame.

Tony Stewart

Tony Stewart is the first driver to win all three USAC national championships in one season, sweeping the Midget, Sprint and Silver Crown titles in 1995. He went on to win the 1997 IndyCar (IRL) title and three NASCAR Cup championships, and as a car owner he has won seven USAC Sprint and seven Silver Crown titles.

Tony Stewart has given back to the short-track world that made him, perhaps more than any other driver. As a car owner he has won seven USAC Sprint and seven Silver Crown owner championships, and his team fielded Donny Schatz's World of Outlaws title cars for 18 seasons.

The Columbus, Indiana, driver won the 1994 National Midget title. In 1995 he won the Midget, Sprint and Silver Crown championships, the first driver ever to sweep all three in a single season.

He won 27 USAC National Midget races, 10 Sprint races and three Silver Crown races.

Stewart won the 1997 Indy Racing League title and started on the pole for the 1996 Indianapolis 500. He then won three NASCAR Cup championships as a driver, in 2002, 2005 and 2011, the last as an owner-driver. He is the only driver to win championships in both IndyCar and NASCAR.

He was inducted into the National Midget Hall of Fame in 2001 and the NASCAR Hall of Fame in 2020.

Dave Darland"The People's Champ"

Dave Darland, the "People's Champ," is a USAC Triple Crown champion with titles in Silver Crown (1997), Sprint (1999) and Midget (2001–02). He retired in 2023 with 62 USAC National Sprint Car wins, then the record, and a record 20 Indiana Sprint Week wins.

Dave Darland was USAC's everyman champion. Fans called him the "People's Champ." Over nearly four decades he made more USAC national starts than anyone, 1,334, and he showed that a local Indiana sprint car racer could become one of the sport's all-time greats.

Born in 1966 in Lincoln, Indiana, he won four national titles: Silver Crown in 1997, Sprint in 1999, and Midget in 2001 and 2002. That made him the third career Triple Crown champion.

His 62 USAC National Sprint Car wins were the record when he retired in 2023. His 20 Indiana Sprint Week wins are still the most of any driver. He won 115 USAC features nationally and regionally, including 30 National Midget and 14 Silver Crown wins.

He won at least one USAC national feature in a record 24 straight seasons, 1993 through 2016. Along the way he won Turkey Night, the midget classic, twice.

Darland is a member of the USAC Hall of Fame.

J.J. Yeley

J.J. Yeley swept all three USAC national championships in 2003, joining Tony Stewart as the only single-season Triple Crown winners. His 24 USAC wins that year broke A.J. Foyt's single-season record, and he also won the 2001 Sprint and 2002 Silver Crown titles.

J.J. Yeley's 2003 season remains the high-water mark for a USAC driver. Racing Tony Stewart's sprint and Silver Crown cars and the Steve Lewis midget Stewart had driven in 1995, he won all three national championships and 24 USAC races. That broke the single-season record of 19 set by A.J. Foyt.

Yeley won Indiana Sprint Week and USAC Sprint Rookie of the Year in 1997. He finished ninth in the 1998 Indianapolis 500 despite a first-lap spin.

After an IndyCar run in 2000, he returned to USAC. He won the Sprint title in 2001, the Silver Crown title in 2002, and then the 2003 sweep, joining Stewart as the only drivers to win the Triple Crown in a single season.

He moved to NASCAR with Joe Gibbs Racing in 2004 and has raced in the national touring series ever since.

Jerry Coons Jr.

Jerry Coons Jr. of Tucson, Arizona, is a USAC Triple Crown champion. He won the National Midget titles in 2006 and 2007, then both the Sprint and Silver Crown titles in 2008. He was USAC's Midget Driver of the Decade for 2000–09 and the first three-time winner of the Belleville Midget Nationals.

Jerry Coons Jr. brought Arizona into USAC's elite. The Tucson driver made his National Midget debut in 1987 and has 430 series starts, among the most ever, and he became one of USAC's most respected veterans.

His first National Midget win came at the 1996 Belleville Midget Nationals. He later became the first three-time winner of that race, in 2005, 2007 and 2012.

Coons won the National Midget championship in 2006 and 2007. In 2008 he won both the Sprint and Silver Crown titles to complete the career Triple Crown. USAC named him National Midget Driver of the Decade for 2000–09.

At Eldora's 4-Crown Nationals he won in all three USAC divisions: Silver Crown in 2007 and 2014, Sprint in 2009 and Midget in 2010.

Tracy Hines

Tracy Hines is a USAC Triple Crown champion with Silver Crown (2000), Sprint (2002) and Midget (2015) titles. He won 91 USAC national races, including 47 in sprint cars, and he won at least one national feature in 19 straight seasons.

Tracy Hines spent most of a quarter-century as a full-time competitor in all three USAC national divisions. He was the son of a New Castle, Indiana, midget racing family, and his father Jim and brother Ted were champions too. He later helped build other drivers' programs as a team executive.

He broke through in 1996, winning his first National Midget race at Winchester in his first start back from a fiery crash.

Hines won the Silver Crown title in 2000 and the Sprint title in 2002. In 2015, after announcing his retirement midseason, he won the Midget title and completed the Triple Crown.

He scored 91 USAC national wins, including 47 in sprint cars, 35 in midgets and nine in Silver Crown. He won at least one national feature in 19 straight seasons, 1996 through 2014.

Away from USAC he raced in NASCAR's Truck and Busch series.

Chris Windom

Chris Windom became USAC's seventh Triple Crown champion with the 2016 Silver Crown, 2017 National Sprint and 2020 National Midget titles. The Canton, Illinois, driver is also a two-time Little 500 and two-time Indiana Sprint Week winner.

Chris Windom of Canton, Illinois, showed how far a family-run program could go in USAC. He won the Little 500 in 2011 and again in 2015.

He won the Silver Crown title in 2016 and the National Sprint title in 2017. In 2020 he won the National Midget title and became the seventh career Triple Crown champion.

Windom won Indiana Sprint Week in 2011 and 2018, the Eastern Storm in 2017 and 2018.

He has also raced stock cars, including the 2021 NASCAR Cup race on the Bristol dirt, and in recent seasons he has moved toward winged sprint car racing.

Logan Seavey

Logan Seavey became USAC's eighth Triple Crown champion when he added the 2024 National Sprint title to his 2023 Silver Crown and his 2018 and 2023 National Midget titles. He did it with Abacus Racing, which won owner titles in all three divisions.

Logan Seavey of Sutter, California, and Abacus Racing built one of USAC's most complete modern programs. Abacus became only the second team to win owner titles in all three national divisions.

Seavey won the 2018 National Midget championship as a rookie.

In 2023 he won both the Silver Crown and National Midget titles. He added the 2024 Sprint title, along with Indiana Sprint Week and the National Drivers championship, to become the eighth career Triple Crown champion.

In 2023 he swept all three USAC features at Eldora's 4-Crown Nationals. In 2025 he won in all three divisions again, 11 times in all.

Justin Grant

Justin Grant is the all-time wins leader in USAC National Sprint Car racing and the first driver to reach 70 series wins. He won the 2022 National Sprint title and the 2020 and 2025 Silver Crown titles, and he has won the USAC National Drivers championship five times.

Justin Grant of Ione, California, built his record on durability. In July 2025 he set the record for consecutive USAC National Sprint starts, with 325, and he became the series' all-time wins leader.

Grant was the 2010 USAC National Sprint Rookie of the Year. He won Silver Crown titles in 2020 and 2025 and the National Sprint title in 2022. He has also won the National Drivers championship five times.

In September 2025 at Lucas Oil Speedway he won his 63rd National Sprint feature, breaking Dave Darland's record, and that season he became the eighth driver with 100 USAC wins.

On Aug. 1, 2026, at Tri-State Speedway, he became the first driver to reach 70 National Sprint wins. He also has 14 Indiana Sprint Week wins, third-most all-time.

USAC Midgets

Mel Kenyon"King of the Midgets"

Mel Kenyon, the "King of the Midgets," holds the USAC National Midget record with 111 feature wins and seven championships between 1964 and 1985. He won those races after losing the fingers of his left hand in a 1965 crash, and he made eight Indianapolis 500 starts.

Mel Kenyon and his brother Don built a family midget operation that set the standard in USAC National Midget racing. Don was his car owner and crew chief, and they also built their own chassis. From 2004 to 2009 their Kenyon Midget Series ran under USAC sanction.

Mel won 111 USAC National Midget races, the series record. He won seven national championships over 21 years: 1964, 1967, 1968, 1974, 1977, 1981 and 1985. He also won at least one race in 21 straight seasons, 1962 through 1982.

Most of that came after a fiery 1965 champ car crash at Langhorne that burned off the fingers of his left hand. The brothers designed a special glove that fit over a stud on the steering wheel so he could steer with his palm.

Kenyon made eight Indianapolis 500 starts and finished in the top five in half of them, with a best of third in 1968.

He retired in 2009 after more than 50 years of midget racing and 925 USAC National Midget starts.

Rich Vogler

Rich Vogler won five USAC National Midget championships and the 1980 and 1989 USAC Sprint Car titles. His 95 National Midget wins rank second all-time, and he won the inaugural 1987 Chili Bowl and the Hut Hundred eight times.

"Rapid Rich" Vogler of Glen Ellyn, Illinois, the son of midget winner Don Vogler, was the most prolific winner of USAC's short-track era. In 1980 he became the first driver to win the USAC Sprint and Midget championships in the same year.

He won five National Midget titles, in 1978, 1980, 1983, 1986 and 1988, and Sprint titles in 1980 and 1989. His 134 USAC national wins break down to 95 Midget, 35 Sprint and four Silver Crown. Only A.J. Foyt has more.

Vogler won the Hut Hundred eight times and the 4-Crown midget race four times. He also won the inaugural Chili Bowl in 1987, and his 16 wins in 100-lap midget races are the most of anyone.

He made five Indianapolis 500 starts, with a best of eighth in 1989.

Vogler was killed on July 21, 1990, while leading a sprint car race at Salem Speedway, five days before his 40th birthday. Under USAC rules the race reverted to the last completed lap, so he was declared the winner of the last race he started.

Bob Wente

Bob Wente was the 1963 USAC National Midget champion, and his 78 National Midget wins rank third all-time. He won the Hut Hundred and two Night Before the 500 races, finished ninth in the 1964 Indianapolis 500 and was in the inaugural Midget Hall of Fame class.

Bob Wente was a pillar of USAC midget racing's formative years, and he raised a family that kept racing midgets. All three of his sons competed in USAC Midget racing.

He began racing midgets in 1954. He was runner-up in the national standings three years in a row before winning the 1963 USAC National Midget championship.

Wente won 78 National Midget races, third all-time behind Mel Kenyon and Rich Vogler. He won the 1963 Hut Hundred and the Night Before the 500 in 1969 and 1976.

In his only Indianapolis 500, in 1964, he started 32nd and finished ninth.

He was in the inaugural class of the National Midget Auto Racing Hall of Fame in 1984. He died in 2000.

Kody Swanson

Kody Swanson holds the USAC Silver Crown records for championships, with eight, and wins, with 49. Those eight titles are the most any driver has won in a single USAC national series, and he also holds the series record of 59 poles.

Kody Swanson of Kingsburg, California, has defined USAC Silver Crown racing for more than a decade. He set records in the series that may never be approached.

He won the 2005 Western Sprint title and the 2009 Silver Crown Rookie of the Year award. He won his first Silver Crown title in 2014, and his eight titles (2014, 2015, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2021, 2022 and 2024) are the most any driver has won in a single USAC national series.

His 49 Silver Crown wins and 59 poles are both series records by a wide margin. He won his eighth title in 2024 despite racing through an injury.

He also won the 2017 Jason Leffler Award.

Jack Hewitt

Jack Hewitt of Troy, Ohio, was the 1986 and 1987 USAC Silver Crown champion, and his 23 Silver Crown wins rank second all-time. In 1998 he swept all four features at Eldora's 4-Crown Nationals and raced in the Indianapolis 500.

Jack Hewitt of Troy, Ohio, was the people's hero of Midwestern dirt racing. He was a racer's racer who would run anything, anywhere, and he was the son of racer Don Hewitt.

He won Silver Crown titles in 1986 and 1987 and the 1985 All Star Circuit of Champions title. His 23 Silver Crown wins rank second all-time. He also won 46 USAC Sprint and seven Midget races.

In 1998 he reached the Indianapolis 500 and finished 12th. That same year he swept all four features at Eldora's 4-Crown Nationals, in Silver Crown, Sprint, Midget and modified, for four different teams. In all he has won 19 4-Crown features, the most of anyone.

He is a member of the USAC Hall of Fame.

Dave Steele of Tampa, Florida, may have been the greatest pavement racer in USAC history. All 60 of his USAC national wins, from 1996 to 2007, came on asphalt.

USAC Triple Crown

The USAC Triple Crown goes to drivers who win national championships in all three USAC divisions: Midget, Sprint and Silver Crown. Only eight drivers have done it, listed here in the order they completed it. Tony Stewart (1995) and J.J. Yeley (2003) swept all three in a single season.

Pancho Carter

Pancho Carter was USAC's first career Triple Crown champion. He won the 1972 National Midget title, the 1974 and 1976 Sprint Car titles and the 1978 Silver Crown title. He also made 17 Indianapolis 500 starts, won the 1985 Indy pole and won the 1981 Michigan 500.

Pancho Carter set the template for USAC versatility. The son of USAC Hall of Famer Duane Carter, he became the organization's first career Triple Crown champion and showed a generation of short-track drivers that one racer could master midgets, sprints and Silver Crown cars.

He won the 1972 USAC National Midget title and Sprint Car titles in 1974 and 1976. In 1978 he won the Silver Crown title, then called the USAC National Dirt Championship. He scored 70 USAC national feature wins across those series.

Carter was the 1974 Indianapolis 500 Rookie of the Year and made 17 Indy 500 starts. He won the pole in 1985 and finished third in 1982. He also won the 1981 Michigan 500.

He is a member of both the National Sprint Car and National Midget Halls of Fame and the USAC Hall of Fame.

Tony Stewart

Tony Stewart is the first driver to win all three USAC national championships in one season, sweeping the Midget, Sprint and Silver Crown titles in 1995. He went on to win the 1997 IndyCar (IRL) title and three NASCAR Cup championships, and as a car owner he has won seven USAC Sprint and seven Silver Crown titles.

Tony Stewart has given back to the short-track world that made him, perhaps more than any other driver. As a car owner he has won seven USAC Sprint and seven Silver Crown owner championships, and his team fielded Donny Schatz's World of Outlaws title cars for 18 seasons.

The Columbus, Indiana, driver won the 1994 National Midget title. In 1995 he won the Midget, Sprint and Silver Crown championships, the first driver ever to sweep all three in a single season.

He won 27 USAC National Midget races, 10 Sprint races and three Silver Crown races.

Stewart won the 1997 Indy Racing League title and started on the pole for the 1996 Indianapolis 500. He then won three NASCAR Cup championships as a driver, in 2002, 2005 and 2011, the last as an owner-driver. He is the only driver to win championships in both IndyCar and NASCAR.

He was inducted into the National Midget Hall of Fame in 2001 and the NASCAR Hall of Fame in 2020.

Dave Darland"The People's Champ"

Dave Darland, the "People's Champ," is a USAC Triple Crown champion with titles in Silver Crown (1997), Sprint (1999) and Midget (2001–02). He retired in 2023 with 62 USAC National Sprint Car wins, then the record, and a record 20 Indiana Sprint Week wins.

Dave Darland was USAC's everyman champion. Fans called him the "People's Champ." Over nearly four decades he made more USAC national starts than anyone, 1,334, and he showed that a local Indiana sprint car racer could become one of the sport's all-time greats.

Born in 1966 in Lincoln, Indiana, he won four national titles: Silver Crown in 1997, Sprint in 1999, and Midget in 2001 and 2002. That made him the third career Triple Crown champion.

His 62 USAC National Sprint Car wins were the record when he retired in 2023. His 20 Indiana Sprint Week wins are still the most of any driver. He won 115 USAC features nationally and regionally, including 30 National Midget and 14 Silver Crown wins.

He won at least one USAC national feature in a record 24 straight seasons, 1993 through 2016. Along the way he won Turkey Night, the midget classic, twice.

Darland is a member of the USAC Hall of Fame.

J.J. Yeley

J.J. Yeley swept all three USAC national championships in 2003, joining Tony Stewart as the only single-season Triple Crown winners. His 24 USAC wins that year broke A.J. Foyt's single-season record, and he also won the 2001 Sprint and 2002 Silver Crown titles.

J.J. Yeley's 2003 season remains the high-water mark for a USAC driver. Racing Tony Stewart's sprint and Silver Crown cars and the Steve Lewis midget Stewart had driven in 1995, he won all three national championships and 24 USAC races. That broke the single-season record of 19 set by A.J. Foyt.

Yeley won Indiana Sprint Week and USAC Sprint Rookie of the Year in 1997. He finished ninth in the 1998 Indianapolis 500 despite a first-lap spin.

After an IndyCar run in 2000, he returned to USAC. He won the Sprint title in 2001, the Silver Crown title in 2002, and then the 2003 sweep, joining Stewart as the only drivers to win the Triple Crown in a single season.

He moved to NASCAR with Joe Gibbs Racing in 2004 and has raced in the national touring series ever since.
