Kyle Larson Returns to Placerville With Four Hangtown 100 Wins and a Co-Promoter’s Stake

The Elk Grove, Calif., driver has four Hangtown 100 USAC National Midget feature wins at Placerville — the inaugural 100-lapper in 2019, a preliminary feature in 2021, and both nights in 2023 — and enters the Nov. 13-14, 2026 running as co-promoter, racing both a midget and a 360 sprint car.

Published November 10, 2026 · DirtSprintCars.com
Update, Nov. 12: The Hangtown 100 was canceled because of forecast storms. Read the story. The article below was published before the cancellation.

Kyle Larson goes into the Hangtown 100 at Placerville Speedway on Nov. 13-14, 2026, entered in both the USAC NOS Energy Drink National Midgets and winged 360 sprint cars on each day, FloRacing reported. He arrives five days after clinching his third NASCAR Cup Series championship at Homestead-Miami Speedway on Nov. 8, according to NASCAR.com and NBC Sports. The Hangtown 100 is the only 100-lap USAC National Midget main event on the 2026 schedule and opens the series’ seven-race Golden State Invasion in California, USAC said.

Larson has four Hangtown 100 USAC National Midget feature wins at Placerville Speedway, the official total on USAC’s 2025 event preview: Nov. 20, 2019; Nov. 19, 2021; and both nights in 2023, Nov. 17 and Nov. 19. The race debuted in 2019 under original promoter Matt Wood. On Nov. 20, 2019, Larson started 10th in a 28-car field, took the lead on lap 35 and led the final 66 laps, finishing 8.152 seconds ahead of Christopher Bell. He earned $20,000 for the 100-lap feature and a $12,000 bonus as the event’s overall points champion, a $32,000 total that USAC described as the richest event in the history of its National and Western States Midget divisions. In 2021 he won the Nov. 19 preliminary feature, and Justin Grant won the 100-lap finale the next night. Buddy Kofoid won the 100-lapper in 2022. The 2024 edition was canceled because of rain and cold.

In 2023 he swept both features. The Friday opener was a 30-lap race that he led from lap 6 and finished in 6 minutes, 14.69 seconds without a caution. On Sunday, Nov. 19, he started 16th after the random draw gave the points leader that spot, passed Logan Seavey for the lead on lap 93 and won by 0.881 seconds. USAC reported that he became the first two-time winner of the 100-lapper and the first driver to win a second overall Hangtown 100 points championship, for a $25,500 weekend. The Sunday win was his 27th in the USAC National Midget Series, tying Gary Bettenhausen and Tony Stewart for 15th on the all-time list.

Larson became co-promoter in October 2025, when Russell Motorsports announced the partnership. That year’s Friday opener was washed out by nearly 3.5 inches of rain, leaving one program on Saturday, Nov. 15. Larson set fast time, started 11th, took the lead on lap 44 and led a race-high 52 laps. On lap 96, Daison Pursley, who had moved into second, tried to pass for the lead in turn one, and contact sent Larson’s car flipping. Larson finished 15th and Pursley won, USAC reported. Pursley apologized afterward and said he had not wanted to win that way, and Larson called it a bummer but said he understood the move.

Placerville has been part of Larson’s career since his teens. He made his first sprint car start at the track in 2007 and won there that August at age 15, becoming the youngest feature winner in the speedway’s history at the time, according to a contemporary Civil War Series report. He also clinched the 2010 Golden State KWS-NARC Series championship there. This year the Hangtown 100 adds winged 360 sprint cars on both days for the first time as a companion class, with $2,000 to win each night, and racing starts at 2 p.m. PT with gates opening at 1 p.m., the track said.
